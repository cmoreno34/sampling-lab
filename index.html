Estudios de Mercado I · Tema 7 — Sampling plan (applied sampling)

Design a sample that lets the client decide. Pick a case, define the population, choose the method, size the sample (the formula does the maths), build the quotas (it allocates them for you), name the errors to guard against, and download the plan. A good plan can say WHO the sample is, HOW it was obtained, and WITH WHAT LIMITS.

1 From universe to sample

Every study narrows the universe to the people you will actually interview. Define three things precisely — get these wrong and a huge sample still gives wrong conclusions.

2 Choose the method — and justify it

No method is universally best; the right one answers the decision defensibly. Ask: do I need to generalise with a margin of error? do I have a usable frame? is the public easy to reach?

3 Size the sample — the formula does the maths

Sample size is a trade-off of precision, confidence, how divided the answers are, and budget. Set the inputs; n updates live. Leave population blank for a very large population.

—interviews needed

Rule of thumb for market studies: n≈100 exploratory (high error) · n≈400 the usual balance · n≈1000 more precision, more cost. A bigger sample never fixes a bad frame or a leading question.

4 Build the quotas — it allocates n for you

Quotas make the sample LOOK like the public. Enter each group and its reference share of the population; the tool splits your n proportionally. The shares should add to 100%.

Group (stratum)Reference %Target

A quota is a field-control rule: define it before, monitor it during, and explain it when you report. Quotas correct visible imbalances — they do not remove every selection bias.

5 Errors to guard against

Sampling error shrinks with n; non-sampling error does not — it can bias a result even at n = 10,000. Tick the threats you will design against, and say how.

The prompt below carries your whole plan. Ask AI to stress-test it — where could it still go wrong, and what would you tell the client about the limits?
